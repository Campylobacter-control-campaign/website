CAMPYLO project officially launched in Taabo

Local authorities, communities and research partners came together for the launch, followed by practical training for field teams.

8 October 2026 · Project launch

CAMPYLO · Côte d’IvoireConnecting research, public health, veterinary services and local communities.

The official launch ceremony took place at the CIE Complex in Taabo, central Côte d’Ivoire.

~40people attended the launch ceremony
5villages included in the planned study

The CAMPYLO project was officially launched in Taabo on 8 October 2026. Led locally by the Molecular Epidemiology Research Team (EREM) at the Swiss Centre for Scientific Research in Côte d’Ivoire (CSRS), and supported by the Wellcome Trust through the University of Oxford, the project aims to improve understanding of Campylobacter infections and transmission to inform prevention strategies for children under five.

Local authorities and communities at the heart of the launch

The ceremony was chaired by André Martin Kakou Brou, prefect of Taabo Department, and brought together approximately 40 participants, including administrative and municipal officials, health and veterinary services, traditional leaders, women's associations and community health workers.

Representing the mayor, First Deputy Mayor Kocoh Jocelyne Sandra highlighted the potential benefits for children's health. Professor Jérémie Zoueu, Director General of CSRS, emphasised the importance of research that addresses practical challenges and benefits communities. The prefect called for cooperation among technical services, community leaders and residents as field activities develop.

Investigating Campylobacter exposure and transmission

Dr Kanny Diallo, principal investigator and coordinator of CAMPYLO, introduced the project's scientific objectives, methods and planned activities. Campylobacter infections are a public health concern, particularly among young children: repeated diarrhoeal episodes can contribute to malnutrition, stunting and developmental difficulties.

The study adopts a One Health approach, considering the connections between human health, animal health and the environment. Research will examine sources of exposure, transmission pathways and the social and environmental factors that may influence the spread of the bacterium.

Activities are planned in five villages in the Taabo area: Taabo Cité, Taabo Village, Ahondo, Kokoti-Kouamékro and Kotiéssou.

Training 26 fieldworkers for coordinated implementation

Following the launch, a technical training session brought together 26 participants, including veterinarians, health-centre managers, community health workers and master's and doctoral students. Training focused on standard operating procedures, consistent field practices, data quality and practical challenges that teams may encounter during implementation.

The workshop established a shared working foundation for the participating teams. The launch and training are not yet recruitment or laboratory results: sampling figures will appear on the CCC dashboard once the site has provided approved aggregate data.
